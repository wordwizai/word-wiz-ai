NAME: The Emperor's New Clothes
PLOT DESC: Two tricksters tell a vain emperor they can weave cloth that foolish people cannot see. Nobody wants to look foolish, so everyone pretends to see it, and the emperor marches in a parade wearing nothing at all until a child says what everyone can see. The story teaches about honesty and being brave enough to tell the truth.
STORY CONTENT:
There was once an emperor who loved new clothes more than anything.
He had a different coat for every hour of the day.
One day, two tricksters came to the palace.
They said they could weave the most beautiful cloth in the world.
They said the cloth had a magic secret.
Anyone who was foolish or bad at their job could not see it.
The emperor thought this cloth sounded wonderful.
He gave the tricksters bags of gold and silk thread to make him a suit.
The tricksters set up two looms and pretended to work all day and night.
But there was nothing on the looms at all.
The emperor sent his oldest minister to check on the cloth.
The minister stared at the empty looms and saw nothing.
He did not want anyone to think he was foolish.
So he said the cloth was lovely and praised the colors.
Next the emperor went to see the cloth for himself.
He could not see anything either.
But he did not want to seem foolish, so he said it was the finest cloth he had ever seen.
The tricksters pretended to cut and sew the cloth.
On the day of the big parade, they pretended to dress the emperor.
They held up the pants, the shirt, and the long royal cape.
They told him the clothes were as light as a spider web.
The emperor turned in front of the mirror and nodded.
Then he marched out into the street for the parade.
All the people cheered and praised his new clothes.
Nobody wanted to say that they could not see them.
Then a little child in the crowd pointed and shouted.
The child said that the emperor had nothing on.
People began to whisper, and then everyone started to laugh.
The emperor's face turned red, because he knew the child was right.
But he held his head high and kept marching to the end of the parade.
