## Read this before you spend a weekend on it

**Almost none of this comes back as a citable credential before November 1.**

Directory listings and review programs run on multi-week to multi-month
cycles, and a listing is not an award. What an admissions reader actually
weighs is Congressional App Challenge (you have it), real usage numbers (you
have them), and named awards. "Listed in the ISTE EdTech Index" is a
distribution win with a 2027 payoff. It is not an application line.

So treat the directory work as what it is: **traffic and school credibility,
not application material.** Do it because it grows the product, on a timeline
that doesn't compete with your essays.

Two things *are* both achievable before November 1 and genuinely useful:

| | Deadline | Why it counts |

| **NCWIT Aspirations in Computing** | **Oct 27, 2026, 10pm ET** | A real, named, citable award. Already on your target list and never submitted. Requires joining the AiC Community first, and an educator endorsement is strongly recommended for national consideration. **Confirm you meet the eligibility criteria before starting** |
| **Testimonials from your 140+ users** | none | Immediate. Strengthens every submission below, and gives your essays concrete detail that isn't a metric |

If you only do two things from this file, do those.

## ISTE+ASCD EdTech Index - the best of the directory targets

Free to list. Register the product through ISTE's **Learning Technology
Directory**, and their team processes it for review. Product pages carry five
quality indicators: **Safe, Evidence-Based, Inclusive, Usable, Interoperable**.

Be realistic about which badges you can earn today: Safe and Usable are
plausible; Evidence-Based needs research you don't have yet; Interoperable
means LTI/rostering integrations you haven't built. Listing first and earning
badges later is the right order.

A premium paid tier exists for featured placement. Skip it.

fit for phoneme-level feedback. This is the single best-matched target here.

**Subject:** Free reading tool that gives phoneme-level feedback

```
Dear [NAME],

I found your [SPECIFIC POST, e.g. "best apps for dyslexia" roundup]
while looking for tools that actually address decoding rather than
comprehension.

I built a free one called Word Wiz AI. A child reads a sentence out

marking a word wrong it tells you the /th/ became an /f/. It's free
with no ads, runs in a browser, and doesn't need an account to try.

I'd genuinely like to know whether it's useful for the families you
work with, and if it isn't, what's missing. You can try it in about
two minutes at wordwizai.com.

Best,
Bruce Peters
2nd Place, Congressional App Challenge (CA-15)
```
